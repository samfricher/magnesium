# Review and transfer

**Use when:** the learner returns to practice, wants durable access to an idea, or has enough local competence to test selection or adaptation.

**Inputs:** exact target and prior observations, elapsed time, known exposures, current sources, available conditions, and the learner's time and intent. **Output:** one appropriate task and a narrowly qualified result; schedule only through the runtime.

## Separate the purpose of this check

**Retention:** can the learner retrieve or reconstruct the relevant knowledge after a delay?

**Selection:** can they recognize that it applies without a cue naming the method?

**Adaptation:** can they map an unfamiliar case, check assumptions, and modify or reject the method?

**Preparation for further learning:** can they use a new resource to improve their solution? Useful, but not unaided performance.

Choose the purpose before generating the task. Neither a new task ID nor a numerical reskin proves that the reasoning changed. Do not require all purposes on every return.

## Retrieve before revealing

Prepare current memory and the selected session before a recap. Withhold answer-bearing notes, keys, worked examples, and method cues from the learner until the intended response. Content withheld from a tool result may still be visible in the conversation or already known to the learner; record known exposure and never claim a clean slate you cannot observe.

Begin with one feasible activity for the current goal and time. A worthwhile composite task can rehearse prerequisites, but credit only the exact component operations actually demanded and observed—not every ancestor in the plan. An overdue item is a suggestion, not an obligation. The learner's current target, no-quiz preference, refusal, or pause takes priority. Treat optional review dates as suggestions; do not invent a universally optimal interval or create a revisit for every term. A gap log in a papers plan sets its own spacing in its header. There, a passed cold redo counts as one of the three passes that close a row.

## Design variation around the missing decision

Identify the invariant relationship and the dimension to change: representation, assumptions, dependence, event order, goal, or context. Ensure the new setting does not require unrelated untaught knowledge. Start with a manageable change; later mix neighboring problem types so the learner must select a strategy.

Teach comparisons explicitly when needed: show how the objects and relations map, then contrast a superficially similar non-instance. For the subsequent independent check, remove the mapping cue. An instructed analogy followed by the same analogy is not spontaneous transfer.

## Example: quadratic structure, not the letter x

A quadratic has the form $as^2+bs+c=0$ with $a\ne0$. The expression substituted for $s$ must preserve equivalence and bring its own constraints. In $x^4+x^2-8=0$, let $s=x^2\geq0$. Solve $s^2+s-8=0$; discard the negative $s$ root for real $x$, then take both real square-root branches:

$$x=\pm\sqrt{\frac{-1+\sqrt{33}}{2}}.$$

The equation is quartic in $x$ but quadratic in $x^2$. By contrast, $x^4+x-8=0$ does not have the same repeated-expression structure. Merely saying “use substitution” would supply the recognition move under test.

Use this as a teaching example, not as independent evidence after showing it. A later task must require the learner to find the structure and verify the inverse mapping without those cues.

## Interpret and revisit

Capture the response and qualify assistance before feedback. Distinguish failure to notice the relationship from failure to execute it; first verify that the new context was understood. Supported success is useful learning, not independent transfer.

Mark a review done only from actual relevant activity; reading the queue or reaching its date does not establish completion. Replaying old work creates no new performance. Preserve original task/work/help in observations and revise current memory when the interpretation changes. Report what changed and which operation was demonstrated; leave wider generalization and future retention open.
