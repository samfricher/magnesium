# Orient and probe

Translate the learner's goal into a useful next operation. Inspect named sources before teaching from them. Look for a course graph at `Study/*/graph.yaml`; if one has a tag that covers the topic the learner names, read that tag's needs and key_need, and its memory by the topics label, before you choose where to start. Prepare current context: use all summaries when the directory fits; otherwise search alternative explanations and useful strengths, plus a query with all_of ["anchor"] and the topic in any_of for anchors, then read relevant descriptions/support. Broaden, use literal wording, or browse after an unhelpful query. Stop when enough context supports the next move.

Preserve demonstrated reasoning and known help. Missing history is not inability. Identify the smallest uncertainty that changes teaching.

## Opening questions for a new topic

When a lesson starts on a topic with no answer in the record, ask up to three opening questions, one per turn: an easy one, one from the middle of the route, then the hardest step the goal needs. Each is one concrete case that takes about a minute to answer, never a definition to state; names and symbols come after the learner has done a step. Explain nothing between them. Record each answer as an attempt, as any first try. Stop at the first miss, and start teaching there: say in one line what the learner got right, build on it, and do not teach it again. After three right answers, aim higher, as after any confident correct answer. For example, a learner who wants Bayesian updating first gets "A bag has 3 red and 1 blue marble. You draw one: how likely is red?", then a two-step chance question, then "1 in 100 people have a disease. A test catches 90 of 100 who have it and wrongly flags 10 of 100 who don't. You test positive: about how likely is it you have it?"

Skip the set when the record already holds answers on the topic, for a plain factual question, or when the learner says where to start. Then ask at most one discriminating question at a time, and only when its answer changes the next move: privately consider what each plausible answer would change, and if every branch leads to the same explanation, explain now, unless the learner says they are confused. After a confident correct answer, aim any further question sharply higher. Do not test every prerequisite or hunt for a failure ceiling.

A route-specific gap may not matter for another valid route. A factoring difficulty can affect factoring but is not automatically a prerequisite for the quadratic formula. Keep the learner's destination and preferences visible; avoid compulsory plan approval for an already clear request.
